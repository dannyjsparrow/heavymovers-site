The job

“Just a few boxes,” said every client ever

You're the new guy on a real moving crew. Joey built the company on a flip phone, a carbon-copy estimate pad and a reputation: no website, no app, and still booked solid. Every morning he texts the job. Then the client opens the door, and the "small move" is thirteen thousand pounds of somebody's life.

What you do

Every piece weighs what it weighs

Real physics on everything you touch. And a crew that won't shut up about how you're touching it.

Early build
Carry

Lift with your legs. Then PIVOT.

A box of books drags. A dresser sags and sways. Couches need a buddy on the other end who moves when you move, and a pivot at every doorway. Clip the frame and watch an $800 dresser turn into $340.

A small move
3,000 lb

That's the light end. Big houses run to thirteen thousand, and somebody always has a piano.

Pad · wrap · dolly

Do it right

Pad the furniture (fold the pads right, Sean is watching), shrink-wrap the couch, bag the bolts in the parts box. Hand trucks, four-wheelers and an appliance dolly that thuds down every single stair.

Load the truck

Truck Tetris

Heavy to the cab, tiers wall to wall, strap every tier. Self-packed grocery boxes that don't stack. A sloppy load is something going over in the back on the highway.

Co-op
1–4

Online or on the same network, with a join code. Or work solo with the crew covering the other end of the couch.

Early build
Drive

It leans. It brakes long.

Drive the loaded truck through town and down the highway: lights, four-way stops, tailgaters, road ragers, a moose, the weigh station, and the cruiser in your mirrors. Pit stop at the Huge Stop for pizza burgers. Or hand Mike the keys.

Fights · optional

Sometimes it ends in the parking lot

Rival moving companies race you to jobs, block the driveway and lowball your clients. Settle it with fists, a tire iron or a dolly charge. Taunts: the finger, a butt, coffee in the eyes, drop the gloves, shirt over the head. Hit a client, though, and kiss the tip and the pay goodbye.

Clients

Every client is somebody

Snobby on the Ridge, chatty in Old Town, grumpy everywhere. Chat them up for tips, pat the dog, keep your hands off Grandma's hutch. The client grabs the other end and walks the wrong way. The new boyfriend tries to lift the fridge.

The crew

Roast them back

Roast whoever you're looking at, and they fire right back. Fill the roast meter and the whole crew goes TURBO. Whole conversations in the cab, a Bluetooth war over the stereo, the boys' group chat buzzing on your phone.

Your company

Start your own

Name, colours and an emblem on the shirts and the truck. Weekly contracts against other crews, and the leaderboards.

Get stronger

Level up

Lifting, care, driving and stamina go up the more you work. Hit the gym, get a tattoo or a haircut (the crew notices). Sell the odd finds to Lenny at the pawn shop. He lowballs you.

Your phone

Joey texts

The job, the pay, the twist. Some nights: "need a favour. tonight. 3am. its an easy one. cash." Don't open the crate.

How it's made

Built on the truck. Finished at night.

Heavy Movers is made by a working mover. Days on the truck, nights on the game, and a pile of machines that keep building while everybody sleeps.

  1. 01

    Real jobs, real crew

    The stairs, the pivots, the self-packed boxes and the clients come from actual moves. The crew checks it and tells him what's wrong.

  2. 02

    Their real voices

    Recorded on the job, then cloned with AI, only with each guy's OK. Frank's is in: over 4,700 lines of him. The rest of the crew are on the way.

  3. 03

    A face factory

    Every night a batch of brand-new townsfolk gets built with Unreal Engine's MetaHuman: different ages, builds, hair and skin. Each one gets checked by eye before it's kept.

  4. 04

    A furniture factory

    Another night shift in Blender turns out dozens of new couches, dressers and bookshelves, each with its own size, style and real weight in pounds.

  5. 05

    No two houses alike

    Seeded households decide who lives there and what they own, from the hoarder's basement to the condo on the Ridge, across 15 job areas plus the town and the highway.

  6. 06

    Tested nonstop

    Over 1,300 automated tests run around the clock in a second test lane, plus overnight soak runs that play the career for days, so the couch doesn't end up in a tree.
